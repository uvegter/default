Module specifications

ECON2110 Labour Economics
Credits: 20
Level: Year 2
Module leader: Dr A. Example (a.example@northfield.example)
Overview
This module applies microeconomic theory and empirical evidence to the labour market.
Module content
Week 1: Labour supply – hours of work; participation; taxes and benefits
Week 2: Labour demand – competitive model; monopsony
Week 3: Human capital – returns to education; training; signalling
Week 4: Wage determination and wage inequality – skill premium; gender pay gap; discrimination
Week 5: Labour market institutions: trade unions; collective bargaining; union wage premium
Week 6: Minimum wages – employment effects; evidence from natural experiments
Week 7: Unemployment – search and matching; job search
Week 8: Technological change and job polarisation – routine tasks; automation
Week 9: Migration and the labour market
Learning outcomes
- Explain how labour market institutions affect wages and employment
- Evaluate empirical evidence on labour market policies using regression analysis
- Interpret published estimates critically
Assessment
Two-hour examination (70%) and one essay (30%)
Reading list
Selected chapters from a standard labour economics textbook.

ECON2020 Intermediate Macroeconomics
Credits: 20
Level: Year 2
Module content
1. The IS-LM model and aggregate demand
2. The Phillips curve and inflation expectations: adaptive expectations; rational expectations
3. Monetary policy: inflation targeting; central bank communication; the Taylor rule
4. Fiscal policy: government debt; fiscal multipliers
5. Business cycles: real business cycle theory; New Keynesian models
6. Economic growth: the Solow model; endogenous growth
7. The open economy: exchange rates; purchasing power parity
Learning outcomes
- Use formal models to analyse macroeconomic policy
- Apply comparative statics to standard macroeconomic models
Assessment
Examination (100%)

ECON2030 Introductory Econometrics
Credits: 20
Level: Year 2
Module content
- Descriptive statistics and data visualisation
- Simple and multiple regression: ordinary least squares; hypothesis testing; functional form
- Dummy variables and interaction terms
- Heteroskedasticity and specification
- Panel data: fixed effects; random effects
- Endogeneity and instrumental variables
- Introduction to causal inference: difference-in-differences
Methods and skills
Students use Stata to estimate and interpret regression models.
Learning outcomes
- Estimate and interpret multiple regression models using statistical software
- Recognise threats to causal interpretation
Assessment
Computer-based project (40%) and examination (60%)

ECON2040 Economics Research Methods
Credits: 10
Level: Year 2
Module content
Topic 1: Research design – formulating questions; literature reviews
Topic 2: Quantitative and qualitative approaches – questionnaire design; interviews; case studies
Topic 3: Quasi-experimental methods – natural experiments; regression discontinuity
Topic 4: Critical evaluation of published research
Topic 5: Research ethics
Learning outcomes
- Evaluate empirical research designs
- Plan a small independent research project
Assessment
Research proposal (100%)

ECON2120 Environmental Economics
Credits: 10
Level: Year 2
Module content
Week 1: Externalities and market failure – Pigouvian taxes; the Coase theorem
Week 2: Carbon pricing – carbon taxes; emissions trading
Week 3: Environmental valuation – contingent valuation; willingness to pay; cost-benefit analysis
Week 4: Energy markets and renewable energy
Week 5: Climate adaptation and the circular economy

Policy report (100%)

ECON2130 Behavioural Economics
Credits: 10
Level: Year 2
